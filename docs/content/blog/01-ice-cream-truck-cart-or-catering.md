# Ice Cream Truck, Cart or Staffed Catering? How to Choose for Your Event

Ice cream at a party almost always lands well. The harder question is *how* it arrives. Search for options and you'll see three kinds of service mixed together: ice cream trucks, ice cream carts and staffed ice cream catering. They can all put a cold treat in a guest's hand, but they work very differently on the day.

Here's a plain-language comparison to help you choose. (Full disclosure: we're a staffed catering service, not a truck. We've tried to keep this fair, because the right choice depends on your venue more than anything else.)

## The short version

| | Ice cream truck | Ice cream cart | Staffed catering |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| Where it serves | From the street, driveway or a parking area | Indoors or outdoors, wherever the cart fits | Indoors or outdoors, set up where your guests are |
| Best known for | The arrival moment and the nostalgia | A compact, charming station | Serving guests where the party is, with the treats you choose |
| Watch out for | Parking, street rules and venue vehicle access | Space, surface and how many guests one cart can serve quickly | Space and timing, worked out with your caterer in advance |

## Ice cream trucks: great for the arrival, but check the curb

A truck rolling up is a moment in itself, and for many guests it's pure childhood memory. Trucks tend to work best when:

- the party is at a home with a driveway or a quiet street where a large vehicle can park safely;
- guests are happy to walk out to the curb and line up;
- the venue allows vehicles to park close to the entrance.

Before booking, check whether your venue, homeowners' association or city allows a vehicle to park and serve where you want it. Many wedding venues, hotels and office buildings don't allow trucks near the entrance, and some streets don't have room for one. Also ask how guests will be served if it's hot, raining or dark, because the line forms outside.

## Ice cream carts: compact and charming

A cart brings some of the nostalgia of a truck in a smaller footprint, and it can usually go indoors. The questions to ask are practical: how much floor space it needs, whether it needs power, whether it can travel over grass or gravel, and how many guests it can serve in the time you have. A single cart can be lovely for a small gathering and slow for a large one.

## Staffed ice cream catering: the treat comes to your guests

With staffed catering, a team sets up where your guests already are (a reception room, a backyard, an office break room) and serves from there. Guests don't have to leave the party to get dessert, and the host doesn't have to scoop.

It suits events where:

- the venue doesn't allow vehicles close by, or the party is indoors;
- you want a specific mix of treats rather than a fixed truck menu;
- you'd rather not have anyone at the party serving or cleaning up.

## Questions to ask any ice cream vendor before you book

Whichever option you lean toward, these questions save surprises:

1. **What's included in the quote?** Staff, servings, cups and spoons, travel, setup and cleanup. Ask whether tax and gratuity are included or added.
2. **Is there a minimum?** Some vendors have a minimum number of servings or a minimum spend.
3. **How long is service?** A two-hour party and a five-hour wedding need different plans.
4. **What do you need from the venue?** Space, power, shade, a level surface, parking, loading access or an elevator.
5. **Does the venue need anything from you?** Many venues ask outside vendors for proof of insurance or other paperwork. Ask your venue coordinator early.
6. **What about dietary needs?** If guests have allergies or dietary preferences, ask what information the vendor can share about ingredients. Don't assume.
7. **Is my date actually held?** A quote is not a booking. Ask when and how your date is confirmed.

## How to choose

- **Home party with good street access and a crowd of kids?** A truck can be a lot of fun.
- **Small indoor gathering with limited space?** A cart may be enough.
- **Wedding, venue event, office or any party where you want dessert served where guests are?** Staffed catering is usually the easier fit.

Whatever you choose, tell your vendor your venue, date and guest count as early as possible. Those three details shape everything else.
